 After applying the region growing algorithm, a single continuous surface may become over-segmented into multiple clusters.
To address this issue, we employ a cluster merging algorithm.
In this algorithm, we first define a cluster pair ( C i , C j ) (C_{i},C_{j}) , which consists of two distinct clusters.
The purpose of defining such a pair is to assess whether the clusters belong to the same continuous surface by examining adjacent boundary points between them. 

 For each cluster pair, boundary point pairs are detected within the specified distance threshold ρ d \rho_{d} as described in Alg. 1 (Step 1).
In this step, the boundary status of each point is evaluated by considering k 1 k_{1} neighboring points and at least three boundary point pairs must be detected.
If the number of boundary point pairs is less than three, the cluster pair is considered to contain noisy or unstable boundary information and is excluded from merging.
Next, for each boundary point, an average normal is computed using k 2 k_{2} neighbors to estimate the local surface normal vector.
If the dot product between the average normal vectors is at least ρ n \rho_{n} , and the difference in mean curvature is below ρ c \rho_{c} , the boundary points are deemed similar, indicating that the boundaries of the two clusters likely belong to the same continuous surface (Alg. 1 , Step 2). 

 Since boundary conditions alone may not fully overcome noise or inaccuracies near the boundaries, additional reliable internal information is incorporated.
As shown in Alg. 1 (Step 3), each cluster selects a representative internal point p i in p_{i}^{\mathrm{in}} , which is the median point sorted by perpendicular distance to the principal axis, and the normals and curvatures of these internal points are compared using thresholds ρ i ​ n , n \rho_{in,n} for normals and ρ i ​ n , c \rho_{in,c} for curvatures.
The clusters are merged only if both the boundary and internal conditions are satisfied.
Once the conditions are met, cluster C j C_{j} is merged into C i C_{i} (Alg. 1 , Step 4), and this process is repeated until no further mergers can be performed (Alg. 1 , Line 28).
